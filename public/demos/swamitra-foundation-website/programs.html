Programme Architecture

Ten programmes, one continuous system

Our programmes are not a catalogue of separate campaigns. They are one system, entered at different points. The National Legal Education Mission sets the curriculum standard; the School and Higher Legal Education Projects carry that standard into classrooms and campuses; Teacher Education makes sure someone in the building is competent to deliver it.

The safety programmes work on the same logic in a different direction. The National Safe School Framework and the National Safe Workplace Standards define what a compliant, dignified institution looks like. POSH Capacity Building and the POCSO Awareness Initiative then supply the specialised training those standards depend on, because a framework without a trained committee is only a document.

Underneath all of it sits the Research & Policy Centre, which produces the evidence every framework is built from and measures whether the framework is working. The Community Legal Awareness Programme takes the same knowledge outside institutions altogether, to the people who have no employer or principal to route it through. Institutions usually begin with one programme and adopt others as capability grows.

    Expected Outcomes

    What changes when a programme is adopted properly

    These are the outcomes we design for and the outcomes we measure. They are deliberately modest and verifiable — we would rather report a real change in one district than an unprovable claim about a nation.

    See how we measure impact
    • Improved legal awareness. Students, teachers and employees who can name their rights, recognise a breach and describe the remedy without prompting.
    • Safer institutions. Statutory committees constituted correctly, trained, and able to demonstrate what they did when something was reported.
    • Better compliance. Policies adopted, records maintained and annual obligations met — with evidence that survives an external audit.
    • Faster, correct response. Adults who know what to do in the first hour after a disclosure, and a written pathway that does not depend on who is on duty.
    • Stronger civic participation. Student clubs, community facilitators and campus legal cells that keep operating after the programme team has left.
    • Institutional self-sufficiency. Certified master trainers inside the institution, so the next cohort does not need us.

    FAQs

    Eligibility, registration, certification and process

    If your question is not answered here, write to us through the Contact page and we will reply with a direct answer rather than a brochure.

    Schools, colleges, universities, government departments, public bodies, companies, NGOs and community organisations are all eligible. Individuals may join open training programmes directly. There is no minimum size and no requirement to be affiliated to a particular board, university or sector — only a commitment to complete the implementation cycle rather than attend a single session.
    Send an enquiry through the Contact page naming the programme you are interested in, your institution type and your approximate participant numbers. Our team responds with a short baseline questionnaire, a proposed schedule and the list of documents you will need to adopt. Registration is confirmed once the schedule and the institutional point of contact are agreed in writing.
    All frameworks, curriculum documents, toolkits and templates are published free of cost and may be downloaded and adapted with attribution. Customised institutional training, on-site delivery, audits and certification assessments may be charged on a cost-recovery basis. Fee structures are shared in writing before any commitment, and concessional rates are available for government schools and small community organisations.
    Awareness sessions carry a participation certificate. Assessment-based courses — committee training, teacher capacity building, constitutional literacy and the Train the Trainer track — carry a certificate with digital verification. Institutions that complete a full implementation cycle receive institutional recognition and are listed in the relevant national network.
    A single awareness or committee training engagement can be completed in one to three days. A full institutional cycle — baseline assessment, policy adoption, staff training, drills and review — typically takes one academic term for a school and one quarter for a workplace. Mission-level programmes such as NLEM run across an academic year with measurement at the start and end.
    Core curriculum and awareness material is available in English and Hindi, with additional regional languages added progressively and prioritised by demand from partner institutions. Community sessions are delivered in the local language wherever a trained facilitator is available; if one is not, facilitator training in that region is usually the first step we propose.
