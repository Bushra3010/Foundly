Automation

What the three settings mean

The difference between them is who is answerable for what reaches a guest, not how quickly it gets there.

  • Send

    Answered automatically. The reply goes out on its own and lands in the thread. A person sees it afterwards, alongside everything else that happened.

  • Draft

    Drafted and held. The reply is written and waits in the thread. Nothing reaches the guest until somebody sends it, which is usually one click.

  • Person

    Left for a person. No reply is prepared. The enquiry joins the queue and waits for whoever is on shift to write it.

Which enquiries may be answered without a person

Each row carries a real message, because that is what the decision is actually about.

  • Is a room free on these dates

    “Do you have a double for the 24th and 25th?”

    The answer is a number the software already holds, and a guest asking it is usually asking three other properties at the same time.

    • Back to a person if the stay runs over a date the rate calendar has flagged
    • Back to a person if more rooms are asked for than are free on any one night

  • Facts about the property

    “What time is check-in, and is parking free?”

    Check-in time, parking, breakfast and the address do not change, and a person retyping them is a person not answering something harder.

    • Back to a person if the guest asks for an exception to the published policy

  • A price for a standard stay

    “What would two nights in a deluxe room cost?”

    The rate is calculable, but a quote is a commitment. The draft is written and held so somebody sends it knowingly.

    • Back to a person if the quoted rate falls below the published floor
    • Back to a person if the stay is four nights or longer

  • Chasing a quote that went quiet

    “Following up on the quote sent on Tuesday.”

    Two scheduled chases are better than none, and better than a colleague remembering at midnight.

    • Back to a person if two automated chases have already gone unanswered

  • Group and event enquiries

    “We need 12 rooms for a wedding party in November.”

    Volume, meal plans and a rate held for weeks are all negotiated together. No part of that is routine.

  • A complaint or a service failure

    “The room was not ready and nobody called us back.”

    An automatic reply to a complaint reads as the property not listening, which is the complaint.

  • Cancellations and refunds

    “We have to cancel. How much do we get back?”

    Money leaving the property is a decision with a name attached to it.
